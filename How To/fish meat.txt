Species-specific fish meat (replaces FishPortion)

FishPortionComponent, FishPortions, the FishPortion input kind, and the old
fishPortionGrams setting have been removed with approval. Cooking and processing
now use ordinary Item ingredients, with species meat quantities measured in grams.

In fishing.json, every fishTypes species needs:
  meatItemId: e.g. carp_meat
  meatName: e.g. Carp Meat
  meatYieldPercent: e.g. 80 (retain 80%, lose 20% while chopping)
All eight starting species have separate meat identities and 80% yield.
Each meat ID must be valid, unique, and different from other registered products.
Names cannot be empty; yields must be between 1 and 100.

Example input in recipes.json:
{ "kind": "Item", "itemId": "carp_meat", "quantity": 200 }
For meat, this means 200 grams; rice_grain and other ordinary items still use
item counts. A single options group can offer Carp Meat, Catfish Meat, Tilapia
Meat, or other species as alternatives. One option must be fully supplied: 100 g
Carp Meat plus 100 g Catfish Meat does not satisfy a 200 g single-species option.
Multiple stacks or whole fish of the same species can combine their meat grams.

The supplied Fish Stew and Fish Rice each accept 200 g of any of the eight meat
items. Their cooking time, servings, quality rolls, food effects, spices, grain,
and meal sale price are preserved. There are no new quality or skill systems.
Whole-fish prices still depend on original caught weight, before chopping loss.

Chopping happens automatically when a fully validated recipe selects a whole
fish. There is no new chopping command, timer, or station in this phase.
Example: 1,000 g Carp yields 800 g Carp Meat. Cooking consumes 200 g and stores
600 g. The next batch leaves 400 g, then 200 g, then zero. The 20% loss applies
once to a whole fish; stored meat never loses another 20%. Usable yield rounds
down to whole grams: 333 g at 80% yields 266 g. Small fish no longer guarantee
one meal; a 200 g fish yields only 160 g, below a 200 g recipe requirement.
Missing any required ingredient leaves whole fish, meat, and other inputs intact.
Prepared meat is spent before chopping another whole fish of the chosen species.

Implementation uses existing PlantProduceComponent item stacks, not a new meat
inventory system. Its Quantity is grams for registered meat IDs. Inventory dumps
show names and grams, such as Carp Meat 600 g. Existing stack splitting and trade
quantity operations preserve the meat item ID. Auto sale of prepared meat is not
introduced; cooked meals remain the cook's normal sale stock.
FishMeat shares species lookup and usable grams. ProcessingRules plans virtual
meat grams from whole fish, then creates leftover stacks only during spending.
Long arithmetic prevents overflow when calculating percentage yields.

The JSON loader indexes meat IDs before checking processing/cooking references.
Processing recipes can therefore also reference these ordinary meat Item IDs.
A new fish's meat becomes a known product through fishing.json, but add its meat
ID to a recipe's options if that recipe should accept the new species.
Manual worlds need registered species meat metadata to convert whole fish;
there is no hardcoded species-to-meat fallback.

AI configuration migration in ai.json:
  old: fishStockTarget (portion count)
  new: fishMeatStockTargetGrams (600 initially)
The cook counts unlisted prepared meat plus net yield of whole fish. Below the
target it may buy a whole fish whose meat appears in a known recipe. One large
purchase can exceed the target because whole fish remain indivisible sale items.
Existing needs, schedules, purchasing costs, and trading validation still apply.
Species-specific costs can still exceed a meal's fixed price; this change does
not establish a balanced or sustainable economy.

Code changes: FishMeat added; old portion files removed; ProcessingRules,
CookingRules, CookingSystem, AIWorkDecision, CookingSettingsComponent,
AIWorkSettingsComponent, RiverFishDefinition, ProcessingDefinition,
PlantProduceComponent, ComponentDebugger, and GameSettingsLoader updated.
Settings changed: fishing.json, recipes.json, ai.json. Program.cs is unchanged.
The old portion C# API and JSON schema are deliberately incompatible; old custom
recipes/configurations must migrate to meat Item IDs and gram quantities.
